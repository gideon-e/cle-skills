**How each entry is built.**

- **Quote:** words found verbatim in the official Arizona Reports page scan of the
  opinion (Harvard Caselaw Access Project), with the Arizona Reports page and, where the
  opinion numbers its paragraphs, the ¶. Only these words may go inside quotation marks.
  The scans were read through their OCR text layer; read the page before you quote it.
- **Holding:** our own summary. Never put it in quotation marks.
- **Decided under:** the version of the rule the court applied, taken from the opinion.
  Say it every time you cite the case.

Pins are to the official Arizona Reports page. The Pacific Reporter cite is the parallel
cite printed on the opinion's first page; no P.2d or P.3d pin pages are given.

Status key: **controlling** = published Arizona appellate opinion. **mem. decision** =
memorandum decision, persuasive only. "Controlling" does not mean the reasoning survives
later rule changes; check the **Decided under** line and the map below.

## Old rules to current rules

Most of these cases were decided under earlier versions of the rules. The 2017 restyling
says: "Prior case law continues to be authoritative, unless it would be inappropriate
because of a new requirement or provision in these amended rules." (rules.md, Prefatory
Comment to the 2017 Amendments.)

| Old provision (as the opinions describe it) | Current provision | Support |
|---|---|---|
| Former Rule 26.1(c) (1992): court must exclude untimely disclosed evidence except by leave of court for good cause | Rule 37(c)(1) | Marquez, 231 Ariz. at 442, ¶ 21 & n.4: the supreme court deleted the provision and replaced it with an amendment to Rule 37(c)(1), effective Dec. 1, 1996. (¶¶ 19 and 21 call the deleted provision "Rule 26.1(e)"; n.4 quotes it as Rule 26.1(c).) |
| Pre-2017 Rule 37(c)(1): no use of undisclosed information unless the failure is harmless, except by leave of court for good cause (quoted in Marquez, 231 Ariz. at 441, ¶ 16) | Rule 37(c)(1), as amended effective July 1, 2018 | rules.md, 2018 Comment to Rule 37: "Rule 37(c)(1) now requires that a court specifically determine that an untimely disclosure caused no prejudice before permitting use of the untimely-disclosed information." |
| Pre-2017 Rule 37(d): knowing failure to disclose damaging or unfavorable information (effective Mar. 1, 1997; quoted in Wayne Cook, 196 Ariz. at 148, ¶ 8, and Rivers, 217 Ariz. at 530, ¶ 13) | Rule 37(d) | rules.md, 2018 Comment to Rule 37: "Rule 37(d) now contains language underscoring the court's discretion to impose any sanctions it deems appropriate in the circumstances." |
| Former Rule 26.1(g) (1992): orders against "a party or attorney" who fails to comply, including Rule 16(f) orders | No single successor. Rules 37(c)(2) and 37(d) both reach a "party or attorney". [CONFIRM MAPPING] | Wayne Cook, 196 Ariz. at 148, ¶¶ 7-8: Rule 26.1(g) was deleted effective Dec. 1, 1996, and Rule 37(d) contains similar language. Neither source names a current successor. |
| Former Rule 26.1(b)(3): disclosures include what can be ascertained, learned or acquired by reasonable inquiry and investigation (quoted in Jones, 177 Ariz. at 412, and Scottsdale, 185 Ariz. at 379) | Rule 26.1(e)(2) [CONFIRM MAPPING] | Wording is nearly identical. No source states the move. |
| Former Rule 26.1(b)(2): continuing duty; amend seasonably, within 30 days (quoted in Rivers, 217 Ariz. at 531, ¶ 16) | Rule 26.1(f)(2) [CONFIRM MAPPING] | Wording is close. No source states the move. |
| Pre-2017 Rule 37(c): a party or attorney who makes a disclosure it knew or should have known was inaccurate or incomplete "shall be ordered" to reimburse costs (quoted in Preston, 238 Ariz. at 133, ¶ 28) | Rule 37(c)(2), which now says the court "may order" it "On motion" [CONFIRM MAPPING] | Wording is close; mandatory became discretionary. No source states the move. |
| Former Rule 26.1(a)(4) and (9) (quoted in Norwest, 197 Ariz. at 185, ¶ 13) | Rule 26.1(a)(4) and (9) [CONFIRM MAPPING] | Same numbers, different wording. |
| Rule 16(f) sanctions (Montgomery Ward; Scottsdale; Marquez) | [CONFIRM MAPPING] | Current Rule 16(f) concerns the joint pretrial statement and trial management conference. |
| Rule 60(c)(3), misconduct of an adverse party (Norwest) | Rule 60(b)(3) [CONFIRM MAPPING] | Current Rule 60(b)(3) covers "misconduct of an opposing party". No source states the move. |
| "Rule 37(e)" dismissal protections (Zimmerman, 204 Ariz. at 236, ¶ 19) | [CONFIRM MAPPING] | Current Rule 37(e) is "Expenses on Failure to Admit". |

The 2018 amendments (Order R-17-0010) apply to cases filed on or after July 1, 2018. For
cases filed earlier, amended Rules 26.1 and 37 apply unless that would be infeasible or
work an injustice (rules.md, Application Provisions of Order No. R-17-0010).

## Link 1: the rules target lawyers' behavior

### Bryan v. Riddel, 178 Ariz. 472, 875 P.2d 131 (1994)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Sup. Ct.; Zlaket, J.)
- **Decided under:** former Rule 26.1(c), the exclusion sanction in the rules amendments effective July 1, 1992 (rule quoted at 476; effective date at 474).
- **Quote:** "The intent is to deter litigants and their counsel from withholding relevant information by precluding its later use at trial." 476.
- **Quote:** "Disclosure, like all discovery, is not a game." 477.
- **Quote:** "Because the amended rules attempt to change a culture of advocacy in which “hiding the pea” has too frequently been the objective, courts must not tolerate their abuse." 477.
- **Quote:** "The situation here called for something less than the effective dismissal of plaintiffs’ case." 477.
- **Holding:** The trial court abused its discretion by precluding the plaintiffs' expert and key lay witnesses. Good cause existed: the amended rules had been in effect only about a month when disclosure was due, and the precluded witnesses and their subjects had already been disclosed to the defense in other ways. Preclusion reversed and remanded. 476-77.
- **Caveat:** Cite the purpose language for the moving side. Cite the result only for the responding side (Link 5), never for a sanction being upheld.
- **Also supports:** Link 5.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** yellow, distinguished on facts by three later decisions.

### Montgomery Ward & Co. v. Superior Court, 176 Ariz. 619, 863 P.2d 911 (App. 1993)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 1)
- **Decided under:** former Rule 26.1(g) and Rule 16(f); Rule 26.1 effective July 1, 1992 (621-23).
- **Quote:** The rules' intent was "to preclude hostile, unprofessional, and unnecessarily adversarial conduct on the part of counsel." 621 (quoting the court comment to Rule 26.1).
- **Quote:** "But the supreme court has directed that trial courts take the time, manage the process, resolve the disputes, and sanction all lawyers and parties found to have violated the rules." 622.
- **Quote:** "While Rule 26.1(g) mandates that the trial court impose sanctions for discovery abuses, those sanctions must be appropriate, and they must be preceded by due process." 622.
- **Quote:** "The heavier the sanction contemplated, the more deliberate the process that is due and the more thorough the findings that should be made." 622.
- **Holding:** Striking a defendant's answer for a disclosure violation, without inquiry and findings on whether there was abuse, how much, whether the party or its lawyers were at fault, and whether lesser sanctions would do, was an abuse of discretion. Answer reinstated; remanded for a hearing. 622-23.
- **Also supports:** Links 4 and 5.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** yellow, distinguished on facts by two later decisions.

### Jones v. Buchanan, 177 Ariz. 410, 868 P.2d 993 (App. 1993)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 2)
- **Decided under:** former Rule 26.1(b)(2)-(3) and (c), 1992 rules (rule quoted at 412-13).
- **Quote:** "the rules are primarily designed to promote the prompt, thorough investigation and preparation of a case." 413.
- **Do not quote:** "untimely disclosure can never be excused …" 413. Allstate, 182 Ariz. at 287, rejected the no-discretion reading.
- **Quote:** "The developing legal culture, however, demands that judges get actively and aggressively involved." 414 (quoting Zlaket, 25 Ariz. St. L.J. 1, 8 (1993)).
- **Holding:** Late-disclosed experts had to be excluded. The plaintiff never filed a disclosure statement, and his explanations showed only a lack of reasonable diligence, which is not good cause. 413-14.
- **Caveat:** Jones read former Rule 26.1(c) as leaving the trial court no discretion once good cause was missing (414). Allstate v. O'Toole later rejected the view that the rule was meant to take away all discretion (182 Ariz. at 287). Cite Jones for the investigation point only, never for good cause or diligence.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** yellow, distinguished on facts by three later decisions.

### Allstate Ins. Co. v. O'Toole, 182 Ariz. 284, 896 P.2d 254 (1995)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Sup. Ct.; Zlaket, J.), but see Decided under.
- **Decided under:** former Rule 26.1(c) and (g), 1992 rules (287-88). Pre-2018 framework: decided before the 1996 move of the exclusion rule into Rule 37(c)(1) and before the 2018 text requiring a specific finding of no prejudice (see the map above).
- **Quote:** "In adopting the amendments to the rules, it was not our desire that substantive legal rights should automatically be forfeited on the basis of harmless human failings." 287.
- **Quote:** "Whenever possible, procedural rules should be interpreted to maximize the likelihood of a decision on the merits." 287.
- **Quote:** Good cause "gives trial judges a modicum of discretion to ensure that cases are not ordinarily won or lost on the basis of harmless missed deadlines." 287-88.
- **Quote:** Good-cause factors include "the willfulness or inadvertence of a party’s (or attorney’s) conduct." 288.
- **Quote:** "Delay, standing alone, does not necessarily establish prejudice." 288.
- **Quote:** "Lying in wait is not an acceptable strategy." 288.
- **Quote:** "The new rules anticipate that attorneys, as officers of the court, will work together to bring about decisions on the merits of cases, rather than try to trip each other with technicalities." 288.
- **Holding:** Exclusion under former Rule 26.1(c) is not automatic. Good cause is read broadly and weighs the reason for the late disclosure, willfulness or inadvertence, prejudice to either side, the opponent's efforts to resolve the dispute short of exclusion, and overall diligence. Court of appeals opinion vacated; remanded. 287-89.
- **Caveat:** Marquez says the 1996 Rule 37(c)(1) amendment was meant to codify Allstate (231 Ariz. at 442, ¶ 21). Since 2018, Rule 37(c)(1) requires the court to "specifically find" no prejudice. State both points whenever you cite Allstate.
- **Also supports:** Link 5.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** yellow, distinguished by later decisions, mainly because it predates the current Rule 37 text.

## Link 2: what you and your client know or could find; verify before you disclose

### Norwest Bank (Minn.), N.A. v. Symington, 197 Ariz. 181, 3 P.3d 1101 (App. 2000)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 1)
- **Decided under:** former Rule 26.1(a)(4) and (9) (text quoted at 185, ¶ 13) and former Rule 60(c)(3).
- **Quote:** Rule 26.1 requires disclosure of all relevant "information known by or available to them and their lawyers. In other words, no more “hide the pea.”" 186, ¶ 17 (quoting Zlaket, 25 Ariz. St. L.J. 1, 5 (1993)).
- **Quote:** "Good faith is not the issue here; it is whether a disclosure was or was not required to be made." 186, ¶ 18.
- **Quote:** "The attainment of this goal is advanced if counsel are on notice that a failure to comply with the rule can rob their clients of an otherwise valid judgment." 186, ¶ 21.
- **Holding:** Failing to disclose relevant evidence under the general Rule 26.1 duty, even with no specific discovery request, can be misconduct supporting relief from a judgment under Rule 60(c)(3). That held even though the court presumed the failure was inadvertent and not in bad faith. 186, ¶¶ 21-22.
- **Also supports:** Link 3 (the client pays).
- **Caveat:** Smith v. Rai & Barone (2022 mem.) holds Norwest gives the opposing party no tort claim against counsel (from the research log; not checked against an opinion). Its good-faith line goes to whether disclosure was required. Never cite it against 37(d)'s "knowingly".
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** yellow, on a collateral-source point unrelated to disclosure. Print no flag when citing it for disclosure.

### Scottsdale Princess P'ship v. Maricopa County, 185 Ariz. 368, 916 P.2d 1084 (App. 1995)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 1)
- **Decided under:** former Rule 26.1(a)(4), (b)(3) and (g), with Rule 16(f) (all quoted at 379).
- **Quote:** The tax court's ruling was "equivalent to a finding that the government failed to disclose information within Rule 26.1(a)(4) that could have been “ascertained, learned or acquired by reasonable inquiry and investigation.”" 379.
- **Quote:** Former Rule 26.1(g): "If a party or attorney fails to comply with the provisions of this rule". 379.
- **Quote:** "Bryan does not stand for the broad proposition that a violation of Rule 26.1 such as that involved here cannot support a monetary sanction." 379.
- **Holding:** A state agency's claim that it did not know the information existed failed, because reasonable inquiry would have identified its own division head. The finding of a Rule 26.1 violation was upheld. But the $5,000 fee award, imposed jointly on the agency and the county, was vacated as to the agency and remanded to assess only the fees actually caused by the agency's failure. 379-80.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** good.

### Preston v. Amadei, 238 Ariz. 124, 357 P.3d 159 (App. 2015)
- **Status:** controlling, **¶ 29 only** (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 1)
- **Decided under:** pre-2017 Rule 37(c) inaccurate-disclosure provision (quoted at 133, ¶ 28), which said the court "shall" order reimbursement.
- **Quote:** The trial court found plaintiffs "were under a duty to ensure the disclosure was accurate before disseminating the information, rather than after the fact." 133, ¶ 29 (quoting the trial court).
- **Quote:** "The record supports the court’s findings and thus the court did not abuse its discretion in ordering payment of attorneys’ fees as a sanction." 133, ¶ 29.
- **Holding:** Fee sanction affirmed. Counsel served a supplemental disclosure of the medical examiner's expected testimony without first presenting it to him for approval; the testimony contradicted his own report. The trial court found plaintiffs should have known, not knew, it was false. 132-33, ¶¶ 25-29.
- **Caveat:** **Red-striped on another point.** Per the research log, disapproved in part on other grounds by Rasor v. Northwest Hospital (Ariz. 2017) (A.R.S. 12-2603 expert-affidavit issue); the ¶ 29 holding was not disturbed and is still cited (Lembke v. City of Phoenix, 2020 mem.). Say this every time you cite it.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** red (disapproved in part on other grounds).

## Link 3: the duty is non-delegable; the client pays for counsel's lapse

### Rivers v. Solley, 217 Ariz. 528, 177 P.3d 270 (App. 2008)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 1)
- **Decided under:** pre-2017 Rule 37(d) (quoted at 530, ¶ 13) and former Rule 26.1(b)(2) (quoted at 531, ¶ 16).
- **Quote:** "the obligation to disclose under Rule 26.1 and the related discovery rules falls squarely on him and is non-delegable." 532, ¶ 23.
- **Quote:** "the trial court expressly found that the client’s failure to disclose was intentional and not the result of any misconduct by his attorney." 531, ¶ 14.
- **Quote:** "The extreme sanction of dismissal requires an evidentiary hearing" 531, ¶ 13.
- **Holding:** Dismissal affirmed. The plaintiff never disclosed a prior accident that sent him to the emergency room 16 days before the accident sued on. After an evidentiary hearing, the court found the fault was the client's, not counsel's, considered lesser sanctions, and found them inadequate because the nondisclosure surfaced on the day of trial. Authorizing others to make disclosures did not shift the duty. 529-33, ¶¶ 1, 13-26.
- **Caveat:** The fault here was the client's own. Rivers shows the duty is non-delegable; it is not a case of the client paying for counsel's lapse.
- **Also supports:** Links 4 and 5 (hearing and lesser-sanctions procedure).
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** yellow, distinguished on facts by two later decisions.

### Marquez v. Ortega, 231 Ariz. 437, 296 P.3d 100 (App. 2013)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 1)
- **Decided under:** pre-2017 Rule 37(c)(1), as amended in 1996 (quoted at 441, ¶ 16), and Rule 16(f).
- **Quote:** "Here, the delay by Marquez’s attorneys was both substantial and inexcusable under circumstances that demonstrated complete indifference to their responsibilities to the opposing party and the justice system." 443, ¶ 22.
- **Quote:** A culprit hearing "is aimed at protecting a party from dispositive sanctions when the fault lies only with counsel." 444, ¶ 26 (quoting Lund v. Donahoe, 227 Ariz. 572, 581 (App. 2011)).
- **Quote:** "the sanctions imposed by the trial court were not tantamount to dismissal." 444, ¶ 28.
- **Holding:** Preclusion of a wrongful-death plaintiff's late experts affirmed although the record showed her lawyers, not she, missed the deadlines. Because the sanction did not end the case, no culprit hearing was required. 443-44, ¶¶ 22-28.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** good.

## Link 4: courts sort client fault from counsel fault; counsel can be sanctioned personally

### Wayne Cook Enters., Inc. v. Fain Props. Ltd. P'ship, 196 Ariz. 146, 993 P.2d 1110 (App. 1999)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 1)
- **Decided under:** pre-2017 Rule 37(d), effective Mar. 1, 1997 (quoted at 148, ¶ 8); the opinion also construes former Rule 26.1(g) (148, ¶ 7).
- **Quote:** "Dismissal is warranted only when the trial court expressly finds that the party, as opposed to the party’s attorney, bears some blame for the untimely disclosure." 147, ¶ 1.
- **Quote:** "The sanction of dismissal is warranted only when the court makes an express finding that a party, as opposed to his counsel, has obstructed discovery" and "that the court has considered and rejected lesser sanctions as a penalty." 149, ¶ 12.
- **Quote:** "Ordinarily, this requires an evidentiary hearing." 149, ¶ 12.
- **Quote:** On remand: "if the intentional failure to disclose was solely the fault of Cook’s former attorneys, what sanctions are appropriate against them personally?" 149, ¶ 14. A question posed for remand, not a holding that counsel was sanctioned.
- **Holding:** Dismissal reversed. The trial court held no evidentiary hearing and made no findings on whether the client shared the blame for former counsel's late disclosure. Rule 37(d)'s "party or attorney" language did not change the rule that a blameless client's case cannot be dismissed. 147-49, ¶¶ 1, 6-14.
- **Also supports:** Link 5.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** yellow, distinguished on facts by six later decisions.

### Zimmerman v. Shakman, 204 Ariz. 231, 62 P.3d 976 (App. 2003)
- **Status:** controlling (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 1)
- **Decided under:** pre-2017 Rules 26.1 and 37(c) (235, ¶ 12) and what the opinion calls Rule 37(e) dismissal protections (236, ¶ 19).
- **Quote:** "We hold that these actions are the equivalent of a dismissal for nondisclosure and must be accompanied by an evidentiary hearing to determine whether the party or his attorney was at fault." 234, ¶ 2.
- **Quote:** "The policy behind the disclosure rules is not to create a “weapon” for dismissing cases on a technicality." 235, ¶ 13.
- **Quote:** "Although any failure to follow the disclosure rules may lead to some form of sanctions, there is little reason to completely bar the use of evidence when no trial or case-dispositive motion is pending." 236, ¶ 16.
- **Quote:** "On the other hand, if a trial is set and imminent, the possibility of prejudice increases." 236, ¶ 16.
- **Quote:** "the trial court should not have dismissed his case without an express finding that he “shared the blame for nondisclosure.”" 236, ¶ 19.
- **Holding:** Barring all of a plaintiff's evidence for nondisclosure, then dismissing for lack of evidence, is a dismissal. It needs an evidentiary hearing on party versus attorney fault, specific findings on what was not disclosed, and consideration of lesser sanctions. Trial could have gone forward on the evidence that had been disclosed. 234-37, ¶¶ 2, 13-23.
- **Also supports:** Link 5.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/8/2026):** yellow, distinguished on facts by nine later decisions.

### Vizzetti v. Ariz. Water Co., No. 2 CA-CV 2024-0151, 2025 WL 1872144 (Ariz. App. July 7, 2025) (mem. decision)
- **Status:** **mem. decision, persuasive only.** (Ariz. Ct. App. Div. 2)

## Link 6: what a complete disclosure must say

### Gerow v. Covill, 192 Ariz. 9, 960 P.2d 55 (App. 1998)
- **Status:** controlling (published Ariz. App.).
- **Decided under:** pre-2017 Rule 26.1 (60-days-before-trial deadline).
- **Quote:** "The rule should be applied with common sense to promote that end, rather than being used as a technical weapon by opposing counsel." 18, ¶ 42.
- **Quote:** "The purpose of Rule 26.1 was fulfilled because Husband had adequate notice and time to prepare." 18, ¶ 43.
- **Holding:** A late-raised fraudulent-conveyance theory was allowed because a continuance gave the other side more than 60 days to respond. The purpose of disclosure is a fair chance to prepare. 18, ¶¶ 41-43.
- **Use for:** the responding side, and cure arguments. **Not for:** excusing a disclosure that was never made.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/15/2026):** yellow, distinguished twice on family-law fiduciary points, not on disclosure.

### Englert v. Carondelet Health Network, 199 Ariz. 21, 13 P.3d 763 (App. 2000)
- **Status:** controlling (published Ariz. App.).
- **Decided under:** former Rule 26.1(a)(1)-(3), (6) and former Rule 37(c)(3).
- **Quote:** "A party is required to timely disclose its legal defenses and the factual bases for them" 25, ¶ 6.
- **Quote:** "If a party fails to do so before trial, it is not permitted to use that information at trial absent specific extenuating circumstances." 25, ¶ 6.
- **Quote:** "Although we agree with Dr. Bennett that Rule 26.1 does not require “detailed ‘scripting’ of expected testimony,”" 25, ¶ 7.
- **Holding:** A comparative-fault theory that appeared in no disclosure or pretrial statement should have been precluded, and a new trial was proper. 25, ¶¶ 6-7.
- **Use for:** a disclosure that names a topic but not the theory or its facts.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/15/2026):** yellow, distinguished four times (Jimenez 2003 on substance versus details); not overruled.

### Solimeno v. Yonan, 224 Ariz. 74, 227 P.3d 481 (App. 2010)
- **Status:** controlling (published Ariz. App.).
- **Decided under:** former Rule 26.1(a)(6) (expert disclosure); current home is 26.1(d).
- **Quote:** "We have encouraged trial courts to take firm, active roles in the application and enforcement of [the disclosure] rules" (quoting Allstate). 77, ¶ 9.
- **Quote:** "defendants' pretrial disclosures about Dr. Yonan were extremely general" 78, ¶ 15.
- **Quote:** "Nor did Dr. Yonan's deposition testimony adequately supplement defendants' disclosure statement." 78, ¶ 16.
- **Holding:** A boilerplate statement that a witness will testify he met the standard of care discloses neither the substance nor the grounds of his opinions; a deposition that skips the key points does not cure it. The mistrial and a $125,000 fee sanction were affirmed. 77-78, ¶¶ 9-16.
- **Use for:** generic, boilerplate disclosure language, including "all relevant ESI."
- **Reported KeyCite (10/15/2026):** yellow, distinguished four times on facts; not overruled.

## Link 7: preserving ESI, and what happens when it is lost

### McMurtry v. Weatherford Hotel, Inc., 231 Ariz. 244, 293 P.3d 520 (App. 2013)
- **Status:** controlling (published Ariz. App.).
- **Decided under:** common law of spoliation, before the 2017 Rule 37(g).
- **Quote:** "litigants have a duty to preserve evidence which they know, or reasonably should know, is relevant in the action" (quoting Souza, 191 Ariz. at 250). 260, ¶ 51.
- **Quote:** "When a party breaches that duty and does not properly preserve relevant evidence, a trial court has discretion to impose sanctions." 260, ¶ 51.
- **Quote:** "One available sanction is to instruct the jury that it may infer that destroyed evidence would have been unfavorable to the position of the offending party." 260, ¶ 51.
- **Holding:** Arizona has no independent spoliation tort; the remedy is sanctions in the case itself. 259-60, ¶ 51.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/15/2026):** yellow, distinguished four times on facts; not overruled.

### Smyser v. City of Peoria, 215 Ariz. 428, 160 P.3d 1186 (App. 2007)
- **Status:** controlling (published Ariz. App.).
- **Decided under:** common law of spoliation, before Rule 37(g).
- **Quote:** "An innocent failure, for example, should be of less concern than intentional destruction or failure to comply with a court order or discovery obligation to preserve or produce evidence." 439, ¶ 36.
- **Quote:** "Other courts considering whether loss of evidence merits an instruction allowing an adverse inference generally require evidence of either bad faith or intentional destruction." 440, ¶ 37.
- **Holding:** With no evidence of intentional or bad-faith destruction, and other ways to prove what the lost records showed, refusing an adverse-inference instruction was not reversible error. 439-40, ¶¶ 36-38.
- **Use for:** the "continuum of fault" (from Souza). It cuts against the side asking for an adverse inference without proof of bad faith.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/15/2026):** good.

### Ferneau v. Wilder, 256 Ariz. 68, 535 P.3d 554 (App. 2023)
- **Status:** controlling (published Ariz. App.; review denied Feb. 6, 2024).
- **Decided under:** current Rule 37(g) (as amended effective Jan. 1, 2021).
- **Quote:** "Ferneau had an affirmative duty to preserve relevant evidence within his control." 75, ¶ 22.
- **Quote:** "In determining whether dismissal is a proper sanction for discovery violations under Arizona Rule of Civil Procedure (“Rule”) 37(g), the most relevant factors for a trial court to consider are" 75, ¶ 23. The eight factors follow: prejudice to the other party; whether the party or counsel committed the violations; willfulness, bad faith, and repetition; the public interest in compliance with court orders; prejudice to the judicial system; the efficacy of lesser sanctions; whether the party was warned; and the policy favoring decisions on the merits.
- **Holding:** A plaintiff who, with his agents, deleted a corporation's emails before turning over access breached his Rule 37(g) duty; dismissal of his complaint was affirmed. 75, ¶¶ 22-23.
- **Use for:** the only published Arizona appellate application of Rule 37(g) found as of 10/15/2026.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/15/2026):** good.

### Fast v. GoDaddy.com LLC, 340 F.R.D. 326 (D. Ariz. 2022)
- **Status:** persuasive only (federal; Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e)). Arizona's 37(g)(2)(B) requires prejudice before dismissal or default; federal 37(e) does not say so.
- **Quote:** "Spoliation arises from the failure to preserve relevant evidence once a duty to preserve has been triggered." 334.
- **Quote:** "Finally, the relevant standard of proof for spoliation sanctions is a preponderance of the evidence." 335.
- **Holding:** An employee deleted social-media posts with intent to deprive and failed to take reasonable steps to preserve phone messages, prejudicing the employer; lesser sanctions were granted and dismissal was denied.
- **Reported KeyCite (10/15/2026):** good.

### Jones v. Riot Hospitality Group LLC, 95 F.4th 730 (9th Cir. 2024)
- **Status:** persuasive only (federal; Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e)(2)).
- **Quote:** "Because intent can rarely be shown directly, a district court may consider circumstantial evidence in determining whether a party acted with the intent required for Rule 37(e)(2) sanctions." 735.
- **Quote:** "Relevant considerations include the timing of destruction, affirmative steps taken to delete evidence, and selective preservation." 735.
