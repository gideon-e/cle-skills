VERDICT FORMS (FAULT CASES)

Introduction

There were no changes to the RAJI (CIVIL) 7th Verdict Forms
Although many jurisdictions submit questions of relative degrees of fault to a jury by
special interrogatories, the Committee favors giving the jury alternative forms of verdict.
Three forms are provided for single defendant cases, and three are provided for cases with
multiple defendants/others.
The first form in each set is for a plaintiff’s verdict without application of comparative
fault to plaintiff. The second is for a plaintiff’s verdict with application of comparative
fault to plaintiff. The third is for a defense verdict. Thus, the jury will indicate the nature
of its decision not only by what it may insert on the form it returns, but also by which
form it chooses to return.
Each verdict form has in its caption some instructive language, i.e., “Use if you find for
plaintiff and apply comparative fault.” The Committee recommends that this language be
included in the caption of the verdict forms themselves, to make the purpose of the forms
especially clear to the jury.
In a multi-defendants/others case, Form 3 is a verdict for all defendants. If the jury finds
for one or more defendants but against one or more defendants or others, it should return
Form 1 or 2 (depending upon application of comparative fault to plaintiff) and place a
zero on the line to the right of the name of those in whose favor it has found.
Standard Instruction 8, the standard closing instruction, should be supplemented or
modified as appropriate from case to case to inform the jury what the various forms or
sets of forms of verdict denote. Where more than one set of forms is provided, the trial
judge should separately label each set and advise the jury to return one form of verdict
from each set.
The Committee recognizes that other forms of verdict might be as appropriate as those
offered in this Section.
The forms will need to be modified or replaced for punitive damages cases, and for fault
cases involving jury issues on willful or wanton conduct.
A combined verdict form has been added in comparative fault cases.
